<p>The population of the realms of this world is mostly human. Most folk in the countryside live and die never meeting anyone who is not. Those of other blood are few, scattered, and never quite trusted. However, the following Lineages are open to players.</p>

<p class="open-question">Our game runs on the <em>Tales of the Valiant</em> rules, so all references are made to the <em>Tales of the Valiant</em> Player's Guide and Player's Guide 2</p>

<h3>Human</h3>
<p>Humans are the people of the world — its villages, its armies, its churches, its heresies. They come in every condition: peasant and prince, zealot and skeptic, saint and knife.</p>
<p>A human can pass anywhere without remark. In a world where difference draws suspicion, that invisibility is worth more than it sounds.</p>
<p class="statline">Humanoid · Medium (about 5–7 ft. tall) · Speed 30 ft.</p>
<h4>Ambitious</h4>
<p>You gain proficiency in one skill of your choice, and you gain one talent of your choice. This talent can be from any of the talent lists, but you must meet the talent&rsquo;s prerequisites, if any are required.</p>

<h3>Dwarf</h3>
<p>Dwarves are a short, broad, long-lived mountain folk, rarely seen and little known. They keep to their holds in the high peaks; those who walk among other peoples are usually traders or smiths, doing quiet business before vanishing again.</p>
<p>However, some Dwarves made their homes about the towns and cities of the Empire, looking for fortune beyond the mountain dark. They are known as patient, private, and slow to befriend.</p>
<p class="statline">Humanoid · Medium (about 4–5 ft. tall) · Speed 30 ft.</p>
<h4>Dwarven Resilience</h4>
<p>You have Resistance to Poison damage. You also have Advantage on saving throws you make to avoid or end the <em class="wikilink">Poisoned</em> condition.</p>
<h4>Dwarven Toughness</h4>
<p>Your Hit Point maximum increases by 1, and it increases by 1 again whenever you gain a level.</p>

<h3>Fey-touched</h3>
<p class="reference">Reference: Elf</p>
<p>Some people are born just slightly <em>wrong</em>: too fair, too still, ears that come to a point, eyes that catch the light oddly. They age slowly and live long — long enough to outlast everyone who remembers them arriving. The world calls them <strong>fey-touched</strong>, and not kindly.</p>
<p>Fey-touched are born to human parents, usually. Families occasionally whisper about an ancestor who "went into the woods for a year and came back changed." Whether that means anything, no one can say. There are old tales about a fair folk called "the elves" who may once have lived beyond the tree line — but tales are all they are, kept alive mostly within fey-touched family lines.</p>
<p>Whatever their truth, fey-touched live as outsiders: tolerated in towns, whispered about in villages, and occasionally turned out by frightened neighbors. Many grow thick skins. Many grow strange ones.</p>
<p class="statline">Humanoid · Medium (about 5–6 ft. tall) · Speed 30 ft.</p>

<p>Involving access to Enchantment spells.</p>

<h3>Gnome</h3>
<p class="reference">Reference: Smallfolk - Gnome option</p>
<p>Gnomes are a small, spry, long-lived folk of the deep woods — the "little people" of grandmother's warnings, though the warnings only sometimes have it right. They are real, but rarely seen: they live past the tree line, avoid roads, and deal with outsiders cautiously, if at all.</p>
<p>A gnome among humans is a genuine oddity. Country folk treat them with the mix of awe and dread reserved for the fair folk. Gnomes are watchful, practical, and stubbornly alive, which in this world suggests someone or something is looking out for them.</p>
<p class="statline">Humanoid · Small (about 3–4 ft. tall) · Speed 25 ft.</p>
<h4>Small Stature</h4>
<p>You can move through the space of any creature that is a Medium or a larger size. You can also attempt to hide when you are obscured by a creature of Medium or larger size.</p>

<h3>Greyborn (Goblin-Blooded)</h3>
<p class="reference">Reference: Orc</p>
<p><strong>Greyborn</strong> are the green- or grey-skinned children of humans and goblin stock. Broad-shouldered, strong-jawed, hot of temper, they inherit their goblin side's hardiness and drive.</p>
<p>They are born and raised in the marches and borderlands, where the human realms grind against the Goblin people, and they belong fully to neither.</p>
<p>The world does not know what to do with them. The Church does not preach against them — but the Chancery keeps track of where they live, and when the goblin wars flare, neighbors remember whose face remind them of the enemy.</p>
<p>A Greyborn grows up knowing all of this. Some spend a lifetime trying to outrun their reputation, while others lean into the prejudice.</p>
<p class="statline">Humanoid · Medium (about 6–7 ft. tall) · Speed 30 ft.</p>
<h4>Heightened Senses</h4>
<p>You have Advantage on Perception checks that rely on sight or hearing. You can see through lightly obscured areas normally and areas of dim light as if it were bright light.</p>
<h4>Orcish Perseverance</h4>
<p>When you would die due to suffocating or gaining levels of exhaustion, you instead enter a death-like stasis. While in stasis you are incapacitated, can&rsquo;t move, can&rsquo;t speak, and are unaware of your surroundings. You also cease to age, and your body is protected from decay. You can remain in this state until you are restored by mundane or magical healing, or your body is completely destroyed.</p>
<h4>Stalwart</h4>
<p>When you are subjected to an effect that requires you to make a save at the end of your turn, you can instead choose to make the save at the start of your turn.</p>
