<p>From time to time, the foundations of the world are taken apart and rebuilt. This is one of those workings: a redesign of the magic system — and, with it, of the character classes that use magic. What follows is an open look at the thinking in progress; nothing here is final until it appears elsewhere on this site.</p>

<h3>Premise</h3>
<ul class="concept-list">
  <li>Magic is not a commodity, not some whimsical "everyone does it" thing.</li>
  <li>Magic should have weight, feel impactful and precious, and needs to be used carefully and purposefully.</li>
  <li>We do away with easy-to-use combat magic. No more fireballs — and specifically no spamable combat cantrips like <em>Fire Bolt</em> or <em>Ray of Frost</em> or whatever.
    <ul>
      <li>If used in combat, magic should be buffs or debuffs, maybe healing magic, or the manipulation of the environment.</li>
      <li>In general, magic should be manipulating the world or, in some cases, a creature or person — but not instantly causing something out of thin air.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li>Since classes like Wizards and Sorcerers are outlawed, and subclasses that use arcane magic like the Eldritch Knight are done away with, we want to see where arcane magic can be put back into the world and made meaningful.</li>
</ul>

<h3>The Classes</h3>
<ul class="concept-list">
  <li>Wizards, Sorcerers, Warlocks and Bards do not exist as player options, at least not in the sense of D&amp;D character classes.</li>
  <li>Same goes for subclasses of other classes — e.g. the Arcane Trickster, the Eldritch Knight, and what else there is.</li>
  <li>The <em class="wikilink">Magic Templates</em> idea:
    <ul>
      <li>Warlocks are not classes anymore. A Warlock instead becomes a story/world-building term for someone who works in service of a higher being that grants them certain powers.</li>
      <li>To address something like that, there will be <em class="wikilink">Magic Templates</em> that can be attached to a character. These are additional character features that involve magic use, access to certain spells (or a full spell school), and some additional abilities for the character.</li>
      <li>Becoming a warlock grants a selected <em class="wikilink">Magic Template</em>, based on the patron chosen and individual selection.</li>
      <li>A part of each <em class="wikilink">Magic Template</em> would be the learning of one spell school (see further down the line).</li>
      <li>There might be other reasons to attach such a <em class="wikilink">Magic Template</em> to a character. Maybe all <em class="wikilink">Forest Gnomes</em> are given a certain <em class="wikilink">Magic Template</em> that grants access to the illusion spell school or something — thus a forest gnome rogue could become a kind of Arcane Trickster again (a rogue that can use illusion magic).</li>
      <li>Bards will become Warlocks, basically. Bard will be one, or several, of the <em class="wikilink">Magic Template</em> types for the warlock template, granting special abilities revolving around performance.</li>
      <li>An example <em class="wikilink">Magic Template</em> could grant some bard abilities (directly copied from the bard class in D&amp;D) and access to the enchantment school.</li>
      <li>Another example could be the arcane trickster, granting access to the illusion school and some other rogue abilities, maybe from the arcane trickster subclass of D&amp;D itself.</li>
      <li>Another example could be a fighter getting into a pact with some entity, granting them some Paladin- or Hexblade-like abilities and access to the transmutation school to buff themselves (with <em>Haste</em>, <em>Enlargement</em>, etc.).</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>
<p class="open-question">Open question: Should lineages come with magical powers and, if yes: how is that balanced between other lineages?</p>

<h3>Magic Types and Spell Schools</h3>

<h4>Arcane Magic</h4>
<p class="reference">The magic used, in D&amp;D 5E, by Bards, Wizards, Warlocks, Sorcerers, and several subclasses of martial classes, like the Eldritch Knight or the Arcane Trickster. Main focus for now.</p>
<p>Arcane magic is actually the biggest offender — specifically the Evocation spell school. The idea is to just go so far as to do away with all the Evocation spells. Other schools, however, are more interesting.</p>
<ul class="concept-list">
  <li>Strip away Evocation.</li>
  <li>Illusion, Enchantment, Transmutation, Divination are all very interesting, and we want to weave them in somewhere.</li>
  <li><strong>Abjuration</strong> is a "maybe". Perhaps some of the spells can be put into other schools, depending on what they actually abjure.</li>
  <li><strong>Conjuration</strong>: yes and no. There is an argument for summoning demons or whatever, but this should only be doable in a ritual sense. Conjuring a hammer out of nowhere, though, is nonsense.</li>
  <li><strong>Necromancy</strong> is much the same as with Conjuration. The idea of bringing the dead back to life is awesome, talking to the dead is amazing — even something like traversing to a "Plane of the Dead" would be amazing. However, spamable damage spells that happen to have the Necromancy school attached feels cheap.</li>
  <li>Besides all of this, even for schools we want to keep in, we have to look carefully at spells that make sense, and spells we don't want in our campaign.</li>
  <li>We will have to do that for divine magic and primal magic as well, by the way — but that is for another time.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Divine Magic</h4>
<p class="reference">The magic used by Paladins and Clerics.</p>
<ul class="concept-list">
  <li>Untouched by now; we look at this later.</li>
  <li>At least there will be some stripping of spells and abilities of certain classes and spell schools.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Primal Magic</h4>
<p class="reference">The magic used by Druids and Rangers.</p>
<ul class="concept-list">
  <li>Untouched by now; we look at this later.</li>
  <li>At least there will be some stripping of spells and abilities of certain classes and spell schools.</li>
</ul>

<h4>Spell School Categories: Inherent, Extrinsic</h4>
<p>Two categories for what we are dealing with:</p>
<ul class="concept-list">
  <li><strong>Inherent</strong>: Spell schools we want to put into <em class="wikilink">Magic Templates</em>, so a character could gain access to them.
    <ul>
      <li>Where this magic precisely comes from might be differing.
        <ul>
          <li>A pact with some entity — pretty much the Warlock route.</li>
          <li>Maybe some angel or even a god itself might grant this power to their chosen (independently of being a priest or cleric).
            <ul>
              <li>Maybe we do away with the priest/cleric classes in the first place and also make that just templates?
                <ul>
                  <li>...and leave the only true "magic class" to be a druid.</li>
                </ul>
              </li>
            </ul>
          </li>
          <li>Maybe it comes from being the descendant of fey folk.
            <ul>
              <li>So maybe it's attached to the <em class="wikilink">Forest Gnome</em> and/or <em class="wikilink">Fey-Touched</em> lineages.</li>
            </ul>
          </li>
        </ul>
      </li>
    </ul>
  </li>
  <li><strong>Extrinsic</strong>: Magic we want to exist in the world, but rather cast in longer rituals, out of a book.
    <ul>
      <li>This magic can (maybe) be used by basically everyone smart enough, as long as they have the book and can read it to follow its instructions.</li>
      <li>This will still be power invoked in some way or form from entities. Most old tomes of magic will probably hold knowledge that's been left by the old gods or something — old forgotten rituals.</li>
    </ul>
  </li>
</ul>

<p>Based on the premises and thoughts above, the schools of <strong>Illusion</strong>, <strong>Enchantment</strong>, <strong>Transmutation</strong> and <strong>Divination</strong> fall under the <strong>Inherent</strong> category, while <strong>Conjuration</strong>, <strong>Necromancy</strong> and possibly <strong>Abjuration</strong> fall under the <strong>Extrinsic</strong> category.</p>

<h3>The Balancing Act</h3>
<p class="open-question">Open question: How do we balance the power level of characters with a <em class="wikilink">Magic Template</em> attached — for example, by a Warlock pact — against characters that haven't?</p>
<ul class="concept-list">
  <li>Does it <em>need</em> balance, or is it just fine to have some characters be more powerful?</li>
  <li>Do we simply make the drawbacks attached to "becoming a warlock" weigh enough to justify an increased power level?</li>
  <li>What do we do with other concepts, like attaching a <em class="wikilink">Magic Template</em> to a certain lineage? Is that a good idea? What would the drawbacks have to be?</li>
</ul>
